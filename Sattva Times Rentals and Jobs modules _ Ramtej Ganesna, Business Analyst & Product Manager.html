The product

Sattva Times is a hyperlocal community and marketplace platform designed to connect local businesses, service providers and people in their communities. The available platform material describes a wider ecosystem that includes business discovery, vendor storefronts, deals, events, job listings, professional networking and service-specific sections.

Discover

Local businesses

Business directory, local services and vendor presence to help users find relevant providers.

Engage

Community activity

Deals, events, jobs and professional networking that support local participation.

Connect

Marketplace journeys

Category-based listings and contact journeys that connect consumers with providers.

The business problem

Local discovery often happens across multiple channels. A user may need to search social media, directories, messaging groups and personal referrals to compare providers. Vendors may not have a consistent way to present and update their offerings. Platform operations also need to maintain listing quality, manage approvals and protect user information.

Available reference documents describe rental and stay-related categories including rentals, hotels, paying guests, homestays, party halls and KalyanaMandapams. The intended journey connects listing owners with customers looking for suitable properties or venues.

One user story, in detail

US-04: Submit a rental listing.
As a property owner, I want to submit my property or venue details so that customers can discover my listing on Sattva Times.
  • Given I am an authenticated owner, when I complete all mandatory fields and submit, then the system saves the listing with the correct initial status.
  • Given a mandatory field is missing, when I submit, then the system highlights the missing information and does not submit the listing.
  • Given I upload an unsupported image format, when I submit, then the system displays a clear validation message.
  • Given the listing is awaiting review, when I open my dashboard, then I can see its current status and any required action.

Process: current challenge and proposed flow

Fragmented discovery — problem hypothesis
  1. User searches different channels for a property or venue.
  2. Information is presented in inconsistent formats.
  3. User manually compares location, price and amenities.
  4. User tries to contact the owner through available channels.
  5. Listing quality and response experience can vary.

This describes the problem hypothesis, not a measured current-state process map.

Structured marketplace journey — proposed
  1. User selects a rental category and location.
  2. Search returns relevant listings and filters.
  3. User reviews consistent listing details.
  4. User follows the approved contact / enquiry journey.
  5. Owner manages listing information; admin reviews quality where required.

Module 2 deep dive: Jobs & Opportunities

Sattva Times lists jobs among its community features. This module connects people looking for local work with businesses that are hiring, and gives the admin team control over what gets published.

Status: proposed analysis. Roles, fields and rules below are my working assumptions for the portfolio. Each one is on the validation list at the end.

Who it serves

Job seeker

Finds nearby openings, understands pay and requirements, applies or contacts the employer without a long form.

Employer / vendor

Posts an opening, edits or closes it, and sees who responded.

Admin / moderator

Checks posts for accuracy, removes misleading or duplicate listings, handles complaints.

The problem

Small local employers usually hire through word of mouth, messaging groups and notice boards. Seekers cannot tell which openings are current, genuine or reachable. Employers get unstructured enquiries and no clear way to close a vacancy. The module should make an opening easy to post, easy to find, and easy to trust.

One user story, in detail

JO-US-01: Post a job opening.
As an employer, I want to post a vacancy with clear details so that nearby candidates can find and respond to it.
  • Given I am a logged-in employer, when I fill all mandatory fields and submit, then the post is saved as "Pending review".
  • Given the pay range has a minimum higher than the maximum, when I submit, then the form flags the pay fields and does not save.
  • Given my post is rejected, when I open my dashboard, then I see the reason and can edit and resubmit.
  • Given my post is approved, when a seeker searches a matching location, then the post appears in the results.

Reflection

This case study demonstrates how I approach product management and business analysis together: understand the broader business ecosystem, identify user and operational needs, turn those needs into structured requirements, prioritise the work, and connect delivery back to testable acceptance criteria and measurable outcomes.

The most important next step is stakeholder validation. A strong product specification is not just a detailed document; it is a shared understanding between the business, users and delivery team.
